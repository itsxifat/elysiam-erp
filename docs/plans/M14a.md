## Scope

The pilot brand starts with books and stock its accountant will sign. Six importers,
one opening journal, one reconciliation report, and a demo org for training.

Everything here runs **once per brand, before go-live**, by an org admin. That shapes
two choices the rest of the plan follows from: the importers are allowed to be slow,
and they are not allowed to be approximate.

## Out of scope

- M14b's deployment, observability and the go-live runbook.
- Importing anything that is not needed to open the books: campaigns, payroll history,
  message logs, historical orders as orders. A brand's past SMS is not an opening balance.
- Editing an opening balance after it is posted. A mistake is a reversal journal and a
  new import, like every other posted journal (invariant 2).

## How an accountant uses it (D-214, D-215)

The console's **Opening balances** screen (`/accounts/opening`), one tab per importer. Each
tab takes a CSV exported from Excel, read in the browser and posted as text — the M06 price
importer's shape, not the presigned upload: a few hundred kilobytes, and the operator is
waiting for the report. Every importer has `POST /v1/migration/imports/<kind>/dry-run` and
`POST /v1/migration/imports/<kind>`, and the commit button appears only after a dry run that
can be applied.

Two kinds of importer:

- **Applied on commit**: SKUs (into the catalog) and customers (into the buyer list and the
  fraud counters). They are not balances and nothing waits for them.
- **Staged on commit**: opening stock, supplier dues and advances, wallet balances, courier
  COD in transit. The commit records the batch and its parsed rows; nothing reaches stock or
  the ledger. A staged batch can be discarded and replaced. Then one action,
  `POST /v1/migration/opening`, posts every staged batch in **one transaction**: the
  `opening_balance` stock movements and the one opening journal. After that, balance imports
  are closed.

Why staged: D-213 opens a brand's books with exactly one journal, and an accountant needs to
see the whole opening trial balance — and fix a wrong file — before anything is posted. A
stock file committed straight into stock could only be corrected with approved adjustments,
after the weighted average had already blended it.

## The six importers

In order of dependency. Columns are named in the dry-run report and in the runbook.

1. **SKUs** (`product, sku, price_taka[, category, size, colour, barcode]`). Rows group into
   products by name; codes are the brand's own (D-219). Existing codes are reported and left
   alone; a product half in the catalog is refused. Written by `CatalogApi.importProducts`.
2. **Opening stock** (`sku, location, qty, unit_cost_taka`). One cost per SKU across every
   staged stock batch, cost above zero, and a SKU that already holds stock is refused (D-216).
   Posted as `opening_balance` movements through `InventoryApi.receiveOpening`.
3. **Supplier dues and advances** (`supplier, kind (payable|advance), amount_taka`).
4. **Wallet balances** (`wallet` = the wallet's ledger account code, `amount_taka`).
5. **Courier COD in transit** (`courier, reference, amount_taka`): money a courier collected
   or will collect for parcels sent before go-live, as courier receivable per courier (D-220).
6. **Customers** (`phone, name, delivered, returned[, cancelled, last_incident_date]`). A
   phone already a buyer is skipped, so history is added once (D-218), through
   `OrdersApi.importBuyers` and `FraudApi.seedHistory` in one transaction.

## How the hard checks are kept

**Check 1, the numbers before anything is written.** The dry run of a balance file reports
the opening trial balance of *that file plus every batch already staged*, computed in
`packages/domain/src/migration` with no database: lines by account, the equity plug, and
`balanced`; and `stockValuePoisha = Σ mulQty(unitCost, qty)`. Property tests hold both for any
rows. A test dry-runs the repo's sample files.

**Check 2, all-or-nothing.** One `withOrg` transaction per commit and per posting, with the
refusal thrown from inside it. The commit re-reviews the file against current data (M06's
reasoning: valid at 10:02 is not valid at 10:05). Database tests put a bad row at the end of
every importer's file and assert nothing changed; the posting test makes the last stock row
fail inside the transaction and asserts no movement and no journal exist.

**Check 3, idempotency.** `migration.batches` holds `(org_id, kind, content_sha256)` unique
among batches that are not discarded. The commit inserts the batch first; a second run of the
same file returns the first batch and writes nothing. SKUs and customers are also idempotent
per row (an existing code or phone is skipped). The opening journal's key is
`opening_balance:<org>:posted` (D-213), and movements carry `migration:<batch>:<row>` dedupe keys.

**Check 4, the journal against the modules.** After posting, the reconciliation reads the
journal's lines back through `LedgerApi` and the stock valuation through
`InventoryApi.valuation` — the inventory module's own Σ(WAC × on hand) — and the test asserts
they agree to the poisha and the journal's debits equal its credits. One cost per SKU (D-216)
is what makes the two agree exactly rather than to within a rounding.

**Check 5.** `GET /v1/migration/reconciliation` puts side by side, for stock, wallets, supplier
payables, supplier advances and courier receivable: what was imported, what the journal
carried, and what the owning module now reports as of the opening date. An accountant
(`approve DataMigration`) records a sign-off only when every pair agrees; the human signature
is theirs.

## Data model

New schema `migration`, tenant tables, RLS ENABLE and FORCE, composite tenant FKs:

- `migration.batches`: `kind`, `content_sha256`, `file_name`, `row_count`, `rows` jsonb (the
  parsed, resolved rows of a staged kind; null for an applied kind), `summary` jsonb (counts and
  totals), `status` (`applied | staged | discarded | posted`), `opening_id`. A trigger allows only
  `staged → discarded | posted` and freezes everything else. INSERT, SELECT and UPDATE for
  `erp_app`; no DELETE.
- `migration.openings`: one per org — `as_of`, `journal_id`, `posted_by`, `posted_at`, and the
  sign-off (`signed_off_by`, `signed_off_at`, `sign_off_note`), which may be set once. No DELETE.

Opening stock becomes `inventory.stock_movements`, the balances become journal lines, buyers
become `sales.customers` — each written by the module that owns it, through its API.

`org.organizations.is_training` (D-223) marks the demo org, and the two consolidated
cross-org reads leave it out.

## Permissions (D-217)

A new subject, `DataMigration`: org_admin reads, imports and posts (`read`, `create`,
`update`); accounts reads and signs off (`read`, `approve`). Both hold `read Cost`, which the
report needs; a test keeps every `read DataMigration` holder a cost reader.

## The demo org (D-223)

`pnpm demo:org` creates the `training` org (with its chart and wallets, through the seed's own
functions), anonymises the sample files — phones become deterministic fakes, names come from a
fixed list, money is scaled by a constant — and runs them through the real importers and the
opening post. Pointed at a real export directory, it never writes a real phone or name.

## Risks

- **A brand's export is not a CSV this can read.** The dry run names the row and the column,
  and changes nothing. No importer guesses a column; an unknown column is refused.
- **Cost that is not cost.** `unit_cost_taka` is purchase cost (invariant 5). Staged rows are
  never returned row by row; the report's stock figures are named `*ValuePoisha` so
  `serializeFor` strips them for a role without `read:cost`, and only cost readers reach it.
- **A second run after a partial failure.** There is no partial failure (check 2), so the only
  second run is of a whole file, which the batch hash makes a no-op.
- **The opening journal posted twice.** The key is the org's, not a client id or a date.
- **Legacy COD settled later.** A courier statement naming a pre-go-live parcel matches no
  consignment; M12's settlement import has to clear it against the opening receivable
  (follow-up, D-220).
