## What this is, in plain words

Every piece in the warehouse carries a sticker with its own barcode. A new product is added
in one form (photos, sizes, colours, prices, website details) with the stock already in hand
and what each piece cost; the ERP then offers to print exactly one sticker per piece, on a
label roll or on A4 sticker sheets. A barcode scanner works anywhere in the ERP: scan a sticker
and the product opens, or, on a stock screen, the piece is added to the list. When a parcel is
packed, each item is scanned against the order, so a wrong size or a missing piece is caught
at the table, and the camera records the packing; the video is kept until seven days after the
parcel is delivered.

Built in two parts: **M38a** (products, stock in hand, stickers, scanner) first, because the
owner wants to start uploading products and stock now; **M38b** (scan-to-pack and the packing
video) after the website work.

## M38a: products, stock in hand, stickers, scanner

1. **The new-product form, complete** (D-601): everything the old website's product form had
   and more, in one form: name, category, description, status; sizes and colours as chips with
   a price per variant; photos (several at once, drag to reorder, choose the cover, optional
   crop to the shop's aspect); the website details from M33 (show on the website, featured, new
   arrival, gender, tags, size chart, material, care, SEO), collapsed by default; stock in hand
   per variant with the cost per piece. Editing a product reaches every field.
2. **Stock in hand at the start** (D-602): the stock the brand already holds when it starts on
   the ERP is entered per product, at a cost per piece (required), into a chosen location. It
   posts Dr Inventory, Cr Opening balance equity, one journal per entry with its own
   idempotency key (not M14a's single opening journal), and records `opening_balance` stock
   movements. This is allowed only while the brand's **opening stock window** is open (a
   setting an org admin closes once everything is counted in; closing it is audited and cannot
   be undone without an approval). After that, stock arrives only from suppliers, transfers,
   returns or approved adjustments. Every entry is audited and shown on the product's stock
   history.
3. **A barcode for every SKU** (D-603): each SKU gets an internal EAN-13 when it is created
   (prefix `2`, the range GS1 reserves for in-store use, then a per-brand sequence, then the
   check digit), stored in `catalog.skus.barcode` unless a supplier's barcode was given;
   existing SKUs are backfilled by a migration. EAN-13 rather than the SKU code in Code 128
   because a 19-character Code 128 symbol is too dense to scan reliably on a 38 mm sticker; the
   SKU code is printed in words under it. Lookup by barcode or SKU code everywhere.
4. **Stickers** (D-604): printed as a PDF sized for the medium:
   - thermal rolls: 38 × 25 mm, 40 × 30 mm and 50 × 25 mm, one sticker per page so the printer
     driver cuts correctly;
   - A4 sheets: the existing 3 × 8 layout and 4 × 10, with a "start at position" option so a
     half-used sheet is not wasted.
   - Content: the brand's short name, product name, size and colour, the price (on by default
     for clothing tags; the choice is remembered per person), the EAN-13 with its digits, and
     the SKU code.
   - Printed from: the end of the new-product form ("Print 24 stickers"), every stock receipt
     and stock-in-hand entry (one per piece received), the product page (choose variants and
     counts), and the stock list (select rows).
5. **The scanner everywhere** (D-605): USB and Bluetooth scanners type the code and press
   Enter. The console tells a scan from typing (fast keystrokes ending in Enter, outside a text
   field) and opens the product at that variant; on stock screens (receive, stock in hand,
   transfer, count, adjust) a scan adds or counts the piece. On a phone, "Scan with camera"
   uses the browser's barcode reader where the browser has one.
6. **Strict stock** (D-606): what is already true stays true (stock moves only through
   movements, never below zero, adjustments need a reason and approval over the threshold).
   Added: a stock count by scanning (scan every piece on a shelf; the count is the scans), and
   the product page shows each variant's barcode.

## M38b: scan-to-pack and the packing video

1. **Packing station** (`/orders/packing`): scan the order's slip (its QR code) or search for
   it; the screen lists the lines; each scan ticks one piece; a wrong item, a wrong size or one
   piece too many sounds an error and is refused; when every line is complete the order moves
   to `packed` through the state machine, and the courier label and invoice print. Who packed,
   when, and every scan are stored with the order.
2. **Packing video** (D-610): while the station is open on an order, the camera records
   (browser MediaRecorder, about 720p, about 1 Mbit/s, at most 15 minutes), with the order
   number, date and time drawn onto the picture; it uploads as a private file (purpose
   `packing-video`) linked to the order, and plays on the order page for whoever handles
   complaints. A recording that fails to upload is retried from the browser and flagged on the
   order.
3. **Kept until seven days after delivery** (D-611): a daily worker job deletes a packing video
   seven days after the order is delivered (or partly delivered); for an order that is returned
   or cancelled, seven days after that; every deletion audited. Storage on the server's disk or
   R2, sized in the plan's risk note.

## Out of scope

- Moving the old site's products and stock (the owner starts fresh; D-561).

## Risks

- Opening stock is money: the posting rule, the window and its approval get the ledger-auditor.
- Video storage: about 7 MB per two-minute packing; at 300 orders a day kept about 10 days,
  about 21 GB. The preview server has 151 GB free; R2 is the production answer.
- Camera and scanner support differ by browser; the console degrades to manual entry.

## Acceptance checks

M38a:

1. A product created with three sizes and stock in hand at a cost posts one balanced journal
   per entry (Dr Inventory, Cr Opening balance equity), moves stock, and offers exactly as many
   stickers as pieces.
2. Every SKU has a valid, unique EAN-13 (check digit tested with fast-check); a scan of it opens
   the product at that variant.
3. Sticker PDFs come out at the exact page size for each roll size and as A4 sheets.
4. Stock in hand is refused once the window is closed.
5. `pnpm verify` green; ledger-auditor on the posting and the window.

M38b:

1. A packing scan of a wrong SKU, or one too many, is refused; a complete order becomes packed.
2. The video uploads, plays on the order page, and is deleted seven days after delivery (worker
   test with a clock).
3. `pnpm verify` green; ledger-auditor on the state change path.
