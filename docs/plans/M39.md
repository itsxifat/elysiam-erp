# M39: Catalog management the way a fashion brand works

Wave 3 · Depends on: M38a, M34 · Decisions D-620 to D-649 (M39a D-620–D-634, M39b D-635–D-649)

> Planned on 2026-10-05 from the owner's first use of Elysium's live ERP: "we don't have any
> option to delete categories, size chart and sizes and others. Also when adding a product the
> price might vary by size or variant — we can't do that. And we also have variant-wise buying
> price and selling price … we need more improvement and advancement here." Investigated the
> same day by a five-agent workflow (an audit of every list screen, a review of variant pricing
> and cost, a feature-by-feature comparison with the old Elysium admin, a hands-on run of the
> real console on a throwaway database, and a completeness critic). Findings:
> `docs/review/M39/investigation.json`.

## What this is, in plain words

1. **Delete works everywhere a brand keeps a list.** Anything never used is really deleted.
   Anything already used by stock, orders or the books is archived instead (hidden from new
   work, kept in history), and the screen says what uses it. A category or size chart in use
   can be emptied first: choose where its products go, then delete it.
2. **Prices and buying prices per variant are easy.** Set a price per size for all colours at
   once, change many variants together (all XL +৳100, these 6 at ৳1,490, everything +10%), see
   each variant's buying price and margin, enter a buying price per variant whenever stock
   arrives, and keep a standard buying price per variant that fills in the next delivery.
3. **Managing 50 products is quick.** Duplicate a product, act on rows and many rows from the
   product list, show a regular price struck through on the website, photos per colour, a
   default size chart per category, a supplier's own barcode on a variant, and a weight for
   couriers.

## M39a: delete and archive everywhere (D-620 to D-634)

1. **References are asked, not assumed** (D-620). The catalog is a leaf module (invariant 12):
   a `CatalogReferences` port lets every module that points at catalog rows (inventory, orders,
   POS, supply, promo, storefront, marketing, courier) register a checker that returns counts
   for given SKU, product, category, attribute-value or size-chart ids. A delete asks all of
   them inside its transaction, with row locks taken first, and refuses with the counts when
   anything answers. Foreign-key violations (23503) map to a typed `IN_USE` error as a backstop.
2. **Categories** (D-621): Delete when no product (any status), subcategory, menu entry or
   campaign link uses it. Otherwise "Empty and delete": pick a target category (or none) for its
   products, move subcategories up (slugs made unique), re-point or remove its menu entries, then
   delete — one transaction. Discount scopes are never rewritten (their terms are frozen): a
   category named by a discount that is not archived blocks the delete, naming the discount.
   Archive and Restore become explicit buttons with a warning that names the counts; the
   hidden "In use" checkbox goes. A product in an archived category leaves the website until
   it is moved (decision row).
3. **Size, colour and other lists and their values** (D-622): a new `is_active` on
   `catalog.attributes` and `catalog.attribute_values`. Delete when no variant uses it;
   otherwise Archive (hidden from the new-product form and "Add sizes or colours", kept on
   existing variants and stickers) with an optional "also archive the N variants with this
   value". Restore. Values can be reordered.
4. **Products and variants** (D-623): Delete a product, or a single variant, that nothing
   references (a mistake or a duplicate draft) — with its values, empty stock rows, price history,
   image links and old-id rows, through each owning module's API. Otherwise Archive (exists).
   An archived product's address is released for reuse when a new product needs it, by suffixing
   (decision row).
5. **Website**: size charts in use get "Empty and delete" (choose another chart or none for the
   named products, then delete); the refusal names and links the products. Deleting a menu
   dropdown says how many items go with it.
6. **Stock locations, suppliers, discounts, flash sales, SMS templates, ad campaigns**
   (D-624..D-629): Delete when never used, otherwise Archive/Restore as explicit buttons; fix the
   location dialog's wrong copy and the 12-character code cut.
7. **Branches**: rename and deactivate (never delete once used). **Expense and income heads**:
   add, rename, archive (never delete once posted).
8. Every delete writes an audit row **inside its transaction with the deleted row's content**
   ("before"), because after a hard delete nothing else holds it. Permissions: the existing
   `delete <Subject>` grants (org_admin), a decision row per subject.
9. A shared console `DeleteOrArchive` dialog: it asks the server what uses the row, then offers
   Delete, Empty and delete, or Archive with the reasons in plain words.

## M39b: prices, buying prices and product management (D-635 to D-649)

1. **New-product grid** (D-635): "Price by size" and "Price by colour" quick fills, select rows
   and set price / cost / pieces together, copy a row down; client validation (no zero or
   negative); the per-variant **buying price column in both modes** (in hand and from a supplier
   — a supplier receipt already has a cost per line).
2. **Product page variants table** (D-636): selling price, regular (compare-at) price, and for
   `read:cost` roles the average cost (WAC), last buying price and margin per variant. Margin is
   computed in the browser from cost fields the role may already read, and the cost-field lists
   gain it so it can never leave the server for anyone else. Bulk price editor: select rows, or
   by size, or by colour; set to an amount, add or subtract an amount or a percent; preview
   before saving; one price-history table for all variants. Layout fixed at 1440 px.
3. **Buying price per variant on every receipt** (D-637): Receive stock and Supply entries take a
   cost per variant (a grid: add a product → all its sizes and colours), prefilled from the
   variant's standard buying price, else its last buying price.
4. **Standard buying price** (D-638): `supply.sku_purchase_prices` (per SKU, optionally per
   supplier), edited from the product page by `read:cost` roles with update Supply, never in the
   catalog (invariant 5). A CSV import and export for it in the supply module.
5. **Add sizes or colours later** (D-639): the dialog asks the new variants' prices (default: the
   same size's price in other colours, else the main price — never the cheapest SKU, never an
   archived one); axis order fixed (size before colour, as on creation); adding a new axis to a
   size-only product offers to archive the old size-only variants; remove a size or colour for all
   colours in one action (archive those variants together).
6. **Regular price and sale display** (D-640): `catalog.skus.compare_at_poisha` (a selling-side
   price, so the catalog may hold it); the storefront sends it, and the shop shows a strike-through
   when it is higher than the price or a live flash sale applies (the storefront's product and
   card reads now include live flash prices — a bug the review found). "On sale" filter works.
7. **Duplicate product** (D-641): copies everything but stock and orders (category, description,
   variants and prices, photos, website fields, size chart) with a new name and address.
8. **Product list** (D-642): thumbnail, stock, website status, price range; row actions (edit,
   duplicate, show/hide on website, archive, delete); bulk actions (show/hide on website, move to
   category, set size chart, archive, delete unused, print stickers).
9. **Per-colour photos** (D-643): a photo can belong to a colour; the storefront groups the gallery
   by colour so the website can switch photos when a shopper picks a colour.
10. **Variant barcode, weight, size chart default** (D-644..D-646): edit a variant's barcode (a
    supplier's EAN) with the existing guard; a weight in grams per product with an optional
    per-variant override, used by courier bookings; a default size chart per category, applied
    to new products in it.
11. Small fixes found: scanning an archived variant in Receive is refused; the draft-pricing
    cancel button's label; the price tile for roles without cost.

## Out of scope

- Landing pages (owner, D-550). Scheduled price changes, website-only prices, a merchandiser
  role (follow-ups).

## Acceptance checks

1. Every entity in M39a can be deleted when unused and is archived with a message naming its
   users when used; nothing referenced by a stock movement, order, POS line, supply line or journal
   can be deleted (integration tests over HTTP on real Postgres, including a race: a delete and a
   new reference at the same time).
2. A category with products, subcategories and a menu entry is emptied into another category and
   deleted in one transaction; the website shows the products under the new category.
3. A 4-size × 3-colour product gets prices by size in one step and a +10% bulk change previewed and
   saved in one request; price history records every change.
4. A Receive stock with a different buying price per variant posts the right WAC per variant; the
   product page shows WAC, last buying price and margin to `read:cost` roles only (cost sweep).
5. A standard buying price per variant pre-fills the next receipt; it lives in supply, never in a
   catalog response.
6. Duplicate product creates a new product with the same variants and prices and no stock.
7. The website shows a strike-through for a regular price and for a live flash sale; "On sale" lists
   them.
8. `pnpm verify` green; ledger-auditor on the deletes, the costs and the WAC path; stack specs with
   screenshots in `docs/review/M39/`; acceptance-auditor.
