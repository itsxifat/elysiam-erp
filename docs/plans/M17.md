# M17: Advance payments and return reasons (Track A)

Phase 2 · Effort XHIGH · Depends on: M16 · Architecture §7, §9, §10

> Planned on 2026-10-02, before the build, under continuous build mode (no approval
> pause; the plan is still written first). Built on `m17-advances`, from `remove-fraud`.
> An earlier attempt (`m17-fraud-v2`) planned a fraud rule engine; the owner removed the
> fraud check (D-291), and this plan replaces that one. Its advance arithmetic and return
> reason summary are reused, without any fraud concept.

## Scope

Two pieces of bookkeeping for the COD desk, and nothing else:

1. **Advances that someone checked (D-013).** A buyer sends money by bKash before the parcel
   moves, usually the delivery charge. The agent records the claim: method, the bKash
   transaction id, the amount. Nothing posts and the COD does not move. Accounts checks the
   wallet and verifies the claim into a named wallet, which posts Dr Wallet / Cr Customer
   advance and lowers the COD; or rejects it. The recorder cannot verify their own claim.
2. **The advance's endings.** Applied on delivery, by the delivery journal that already
   exists. On a cancel, refunded or kept per the brand's policy; on a return, the same with
   its own policy. A refund is owed at the ending and posted when accounts records that the
   money went back. This closes M10's two follow-ups: no refund path, and one advance per
   order (D-101).
3. **Return reasons.** A reason code from a fixed, translated list on every return and every
   exchange, and a report of returns by reason over a date range.

## Out of scope

- A payment gateway (D-013 says optional, later). No bKash API call of any kind.
- Anything from the removed fraud check: rules, trust bands, `require_advance`, address
  signals. Confirming an order does not look at advances.
- Store credit: an exchange credit (M10) stays as it is, with no refund path of its own.
- A settings screen for the advance policy: the keys live in `org.settings`, like the courier
  booking policy, until a settings screen exists.
- POS returns (D-169): the till's return flow is its own and is not touched.
- Return rates by zone or channel. The report is by reason.

## Data model

One generated migration (`m17_advances_returns_schema`) and one custom (`m17_advances_returns_policies`,
RLS, grants and triggers). Both tables are in `sales`, with `org_id`, RLS ENABLE and FORCE,
composite tenant foreign keys and minimal grants.

- `sales.order_advances`: order, method (`bkash`, `nagad`, `bank`), transaction reference
  (normalised), amount, status, wallet account, applied amount, the received and settled
  journal ids, who recorded, decided and settled it, and when.
  - Status: `pending` → `verified` | `rejected`; `verified` → `applied` | `refund_due` |
    `forfeited`; `refund_due` → `refunded`. A verification on an order that already ended
    goes straight from `pending` to its ending.
  - CHECKs: amount > 0; 0 ≤ applied ≤ amount; a decided claim has a decider who is not the
    recorder; every status past `pending`/`rejected` has its received journal, and those two
    never do; a refunded or forfeited advance has its settled journal.
  - A partial unique index: one live claim per (org, method, reference). A rejected claim
    frees its reference.
  - A trigger refuses DELETE, any change to the claim's terms, and any status move off the
    list above.
- `sales.order_returns`: one row per order: kind (`return`, `exchange`), reason code, note,
  business date, who. INSERT and SELECT only. A deferred constraint trigger on
  `sales.orders` refuses a move into `returned` or `exchange` that has no row by commit.

## Domain functions and what tests them

In `packages/domain/src/orders`:

- `advances.ts`:
  - `normalizeTransactionRef` (Bangla digits, spaces, dashes, case).
  - `advanceVerification({ status, parcelLive, policy })`: apply to the COD, settle now (and
    how), or wait. Table test over every status.
  - `allocateApplied(advances, applied, otherCredit)`: what each advance gave to a delivery,
    oldest first, after an exchange credit. Property: the shares sum to what the advances
    paid, never more than any one held.
  - `settlementsFor(advances, disposition)`: the remainder of each, per the policy.
  - `parseAdvancePolicy` and the defaults (refund on cancel, keep on return).
- `returns.ts`: the reason codes and `summarizeReturnReasons(rows)`, shares in basis points
  that sum to exactly 10,000 (largest remainder). Table and property tests.
- `state.ts`: three new effects. `apply_advances` after the delivery postings,
  `settle_advances` on every cancel and every received return, `record_return_reason` on
  `receive_return` and `exchange`. `receive_return` and `exchange` refuse without a reason
  code (`ORDER_RETURN_REASON_REQUIRED`). The exhaustive pair test still holds.
- `posting/customer-advance.ts`: one journal per advance, keyed
  `customer_advance:<advance id>:received | refunded | forfeited`. A forfeit credits Other
  income. Worked examples and a balance property.

## API, events and screens

Orders module:

- `GET /v1/orders/:id/advances` (`read Order`, row scope): the order's claims.
- `POST /v1/orders/:id/advances` (`update Order`, row scope): record a claim.
- `GET /v1/order-advances?status=` (`create Wallet`): the accounts queue, pending and refund
  due by default.
- `POST /v1/order-advances/:id/verify` (`create Wallet`): names the wallet; posts.
- `POST /v1/order-advances/:id/reject` (`create Wallet`): needs a reason.
- `POST /v1/order-advances/:id/refund` (`create Wallet`): records the money going back; posts.
- `GET /v1/orders/return-reasons?from&to` (`read Order`, row scope): the report.
- `POST /v1/orders/:id/receive-return` and `POST /v1/orders/:id/exchange` take
  `returnReason`; so does `POST /v1/courier/parcels/:id/receive-return`, through the orders
  port.
- **Removed:** `POST /v1/orders/:id/advance`, which posted on one person's say-so, and
  `advancePaidPoisha` on order create, which lowered the COD with no journal behind it at all.
  Both are ways for an unverified advance to reach the books (check 2).

Console, in the existing style, every string in bn and en, every amount through `<Money>`:

- Order detail: an Advances panel (the claims, and a record dialog).
- Accounts: an Advances page, the verification and refund queue.
- Courier returns: a required reason select in the receive dialog.
- Orders: a Returns by reason report with a date range.

## Decisions to record

D-230 to D-235, in short:

- D-230: claims, verification, several advances per order, one live claim per reference,
  the recorder cannot verify, the two old paths removed. Supersedes D-101.
- D-231: when a verification may lower the COD, and what it does on an order in flight or
  ended.
- D-232 (ASSUMED): the policy keys and defaults; a refund is owed at the ending and posted
  when it is paid.
- D-233: applied on delivery, oldest first after any exchange credit; a partial delivery's
  remainder is settled at the return.
- D-234: return reasons: the fixed list, required on every return and exchange, the database
  floor, the report.
- D-235: the grants.

## Risks

- **Money in the books that nobody checked.** The claim writes no journal and leaves the COD
  alone. Only verification posts, by a different person holding `create Wallet`, and the
  database refuses a verified row without its journal.
- **The COD and the books drifting apart.** Verification raises the order's advance and posts
  in one transaction, with the COD CHECK under it. The golden-books test compares the
  customer-advance balance with the advance rows at every step.
- **A refund or forfeit posted twice.** Each is keyed per advance, the status moves under a
  conditional UPDATE, and the trigger allows each ending once.
- **One bKash payment claimed twice.** The partial unique index refuses it.
- **A parcel already with the courier.** Its COD is fixed at booking, so verification waits
  rather than lowering a figure the rider never sees.
- **A return with no reason.** Refused by the machine, and by a deferred trigger if anything
  bypasses the machine.
