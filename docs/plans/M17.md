> Planned on 2026-10-02, before the build, under continuous build mode (no approval
> pause; the plan is still written first). Built on `m17-fraud-v2`, stacked on
> `m16-buyer-crm`.

## Scope

M13 scores a phone number. M17 turns that score into a brand's own policy and makes the
one policy with money in it, "pay an advance first", actually work end to end:

1. **Rules a brand writes.** A per-org rule list. Each rule's conditions are ANDed:
   delivery zone, order value range, trust band, first-time buyer, and a repeat-return
   address. A matching rule decides `warn`, `require_advance` or `block`. Rules only
   make a decision stricter; the override is the only way down (D-232).
2. **Address signals.** A buyer who has been refused at a door often comes back with a
   new SIM and the same address. Each assessed order's normalised address is kept per
   brand, and pg_trgm similarity finds earlier returns at "the same" address from a
   different number (D-231).
3. **Advances that someone checked.** The agent records the bKash transaction id and
   amount, and nothing posts. Accounts verifies it into a named wallet, which posts
   Dr Wallet / Cr Customer advance and lowers the order's COD (D-013, D-234). A buyer
   may pay more than once, which closes M10's one-advance follow-up (D-101).
4. **`require_advance` holds the confirm** until the verified advances cover what the
   decision asks for: the delivery charge, or a rule's fixed amount (D-233).
5. **The advance's three endings.** Applied on delivery (the existing delivery journal).
   Refunded or forfeited on cancel, per the brand's policy (D-235). Forfeited or refunded
   when the parcel comes back (D-236). This closes M10's "no refund path" follow-up.
6. **Return reasons.** A reason code and note on a returned order, and a summary by
   reason, zone and channel (D-237).

## Out of scope

- **New fraud providers.** No Bangladeshi fraud-check service publishes documentation
  that can be read without an account, and the community packages work by signing in to
  courier merchant portals. Nothing is built against an invented endpoint (D-230).
- A payment gateway for advances (D-013 says optional, later).
- Using a buyer's leftover credit on a later order (a store-credit wallet).
- Cross-brand address signals. D-032 keeps the phone hash as the only cross-org link.
- Per-brand return-reason lists. The list is fixed and translated (D-237).

## Data model

Two migrations, named `m17_*`, plus a custom one for policies, grants and triggers.

- `fraud.order_rules` (tenant). Columns: name, position, enabled; conditions
  `zone_ids uuid[]`, `order_value_min_poisha`, `order_value_max_poisha`,
  `trust_bands text[]`, `first_time_buyer`, `repeat_return_address`; the decision; and
  `advance_fixed_poisha` (null means the delivery charge). CHECKs keep min ≤ max, the
  band list inside §10's four, and a fixed amount only on `require_advance`.
- `fraud.rules` (existing) gains `address_min_returns` (default 2) and
  `address_lookback_days` (default 180).
- `fraud.order_addresses` (tenant). One row per order: phone hash, normalised address,
  outcome and when. GIN `gin_trgm_ops` index on the address. Written by the confirm
  guard, like `order_phones`; the outcome comes from the existing subscriber. A trigger
  lets only the outcome change. pg_trgm is already installed (0025, 0038).
- `sales.order_advances` (tenant). Columns: order, method (`bkash`, `nagad`, `bank`),
  transaction reference, amount, status, wallet, applied and settled amounts, the
  received and settled journal ids, and who recorded, decided and settled it.
  - Status: `pending` → `verified` | `rejected` | `void`, then `verified` → `applied` |
    `refunded` | `forfeited`.
  - A partial unique index allows one live claim per (org, method, reference).
  - CHECKs: the verifier is not the recorder, and applied + settled ≤ amount.
  - A trigger refuses any other transition and any change to the claim's terms.
- `sales.order_return_reasons` (tenant). One row per order: code, note, who and when.
- `org.settings` keys `orders.advance_on_cancel` (`refund` | `forfeit`) and
  `orders.advance_on_return` (`forfeit` | `refund`).

## Domain functions and what tests them

- `fraud/rules.ts`: `validateOrderRule`, `evaluateOrderRules(rules, facts)`, and the
  escalation order. Table tests for every condition, and properties: the result is never
  milder than the band decision, rule order does not matter, and a disabled rule never
  matches (check 1).
- `fraud/address.ts`: `normalizeAddress`. It handles Bangla digits, case, punctuation and
  common abbreviations (`h`/`house`, `rd`/`road`, `sec`/`sector`). Table tests.
- `fraud/score.ts`: an optional `address` input. A repeat-return address costs 20 points
  and appears as reason `repeat_return_address`. Existing tests stay green.
- `fraud/advance.ts`:
  - `requiredAdvancePoisha(requirement, deliveryCharge)` and `advanceGate(...)`.
  - `allocateApplied(advances, applied)`, oldest first, with property tests.
  - `settlementFor(advance, policy)`.
- `orders/state.ts`:
  - A confirm on `require_advance` is refused (`ORDER_ADVANCE_REQUIRED`) unless the
    verified advances cover the requirement or an override reason is present.
  - New effects: `settle_advances` on every cancel and on `receive_return`, and
    `apply_advances` on deliveries. The exhaustive pair test is updated.
- `orders/returns.ts`: the reason codes and `summarizeReturns(rows)`, with shares in
  basis points that sum exactly. Table and property tests.
- `posting/customer-advance.ts`:
  - Keyed per advance (`order_advance:<id>:received | refunded | forfeited`).
  - A `forfeited` direction credits Other income.
  - Balance properties and worked examples.

## API, events and screens

Fraud module:

- `GET /v1/fraud/order-rules` (`read FraudSignal`).
- `POST /v1/fraud/order-rules`, `PATCH /v1/fraud/order-rules/:id` and
  `DELETE /v1/fraud/order-rules/:id` (`update FraudOverride`, audited).
- `GET/PUT /v1/fraud/rules` (existing) carry the two address settings.
- The `FRAUD_GATE` port gains `assessOrder` (a read-only preview) and takes the order
  facts: zone, value, delivery charge, first-time flag, verified advance, and address.

Orders module:

- `POST /v1/orders/:id/advances` (`update Order`, row scope) records a claim.
- `GET /v1/orders/:id/advances` (`read Order`) returns the order's claims plus the
  requirement and whether it is met.
- `GET /v1/order-advances?status=` (`create Wallet`) is the verification queue.
- `POST /v1/order-advances/:id/verify` (`create Wallet`) posts the advance; its body
  names the wallet. `POST /v1/order-advances/:id/reject` (`create Wallet`) needs a reason.
- `GET/PUT /v1/orders/advance-policy` (`read Order` / `update FraudOverride`).
- `PUT /v1/orders/:id/return-reason` (`update Order`, row scope).
- `GET /v1/orders/returns/summary?from&to` (`read Order`, row scope).
- **Removed:** `POST /v1/orders/:id/advance`, which posted on the agent's say-so (D-234).

Console: a "Risk" section (`/risk`) with three tabs.

- Rules: bands, address settings, the rule list with an editor, and the advance policy.
- Advances: the verification queue.
- Returns: reason analytics.

The order detail gains two panels. Advances shows the requirement, the claims and a
record form. Return reason shows a selector and a note. Every string is in bn and en,
and every amount is shown with `<Money>`.

## Risks

- **Money posted on an unverified claim.** The claim writes no journal. Only
  verification posts, and it is a different person with `create Wallet`.
- **The order's advance column drifting from the books.** Verification raises
  `advance_paid_poisha` and posts in one transaction. The golden-books test compares
  the column, the claims and the customer-advance balance at every step.
- **A refund posted twice.** Each settlement is keyed per advance. The row's status
  moves under a conditional UPDATE, and the trigger allows `verified` → settled once.
- **A transaction id reused for two orders.** The partial unique index refuses it,
  and so does the service before it.
- **A rule that loosens a block.** It is impossible by construction (D-232), and a
  property test asserts it.
- **False address matches.** The match needs a similar address _and_ N returns from
  other numbers within the lookback. The threshold is tested against seeded near-miss
  addresses.
