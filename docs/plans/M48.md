## What this is, in plain words

1. **Several numbers per person.** The buyer page has a Numbers card: each number with its place
   (primary, no. 2, no. 3…), what kind of line it is, whose it is (their own, the home's, a
   relative's, work, someone else's) and the staff's details on why and when they use it. Staff add
   a number only with those details; a number the system added (an order, the website, a chat)
   says "Needs details" until someone writes them. Make primary, Edit, and Retire with a reason
   (a changed or recycled SIM, a wrong number): a retired number finds nobody but stays in the
   history.
2. **One number, several people.** A home phone, relatives or a shop's phone can be on several
   people, each with their own relation and note. At the desk and in the inbox's order form, a
   number several people use lists them all: the agent picks who is ordering or adds a new person
   on that number. An order that arrives by itself (the website, ncom) goes to the person whose
   own primary number it is, else to whoever ordered last, and is flagged "Check buyer" on the desk
   until someone confirms ("Buyer is right") or a supervisor changes it.
3. **Orders for someone else.** The desk's "For someone else": the buyer is who ordered; the
   parcel goes to the recipient's name, phone and address, with a note ("son ordering for his
   mother"). The recipient can be saved as a customer, and their number added to the buyer's only
   when ticked. ncom orders with two different phones become such orders by themselves. The order
   page shows Buyer and Recipient apart, each with a Call button.
4. **Correcting mistakes.** A supervisor can change an order's buyer (its counts move with it) and
   merge two records of the same person (numbers, addresses, orders, notes and calls move to the
   kept one; the other is kept as "merged" and never deleted).
5. **The caller panel** lists the person's numbers with their notes and, when the calling number
   is several people's, all of them with a switch.
